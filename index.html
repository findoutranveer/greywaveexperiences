Why we exist

Travel is not about seeing more places. It is about seeing one place properly.

Most Jaipur itineraries are a race. Six sights before lunch, a scripted commentary, and two unscheduled stops at shops that pay for the privilege of your attention. You leave having photographed a city you never actually met.

Greywave was built the other way round. We run a small number of journeys, slowly, in a single region we know by heart. Our guides are trained heritage historians on a flat professional salary paid by us — never by a shop, never by a hotel, never by a commission. What they tell you is what they believe.

We are made for travellers who would rather understand one courtyard than photograph twenty.

The ridgeline

Three forts, one afternoon apart

Slot — Amer Fort
01

Amer

Hilltop palace · begun 1592

Most visitors see four courtyards and leave. We take the corridors behind them — the servants' passages, the winter apartments, the mirror work in the Sheesh Mahal read as engineering rather than decoration. Our historians are licensed to interpret the architecture, and they will tell you which restorations are honest and which are not.

Visit Amer with us
Slot — Jaigarh Fort
02

Jaigarh

Aravalli ridge · the arsenal

The fort that was never taken. Jaigarh held the foundry that armed the whole kingdom, and still holds Jaivana — for two centuries the largest wheeled cannon on earth. Underneath it runs the subterranean passage down to Amer, built so the treasury could move without ever surfacing. We walk you through both.

Visit Jaigarh with us
Slot — Nahargarh Fort
03

Nahargarh

700 feet above the walled city

The retreat rather than the stronghold. Nine identical apartments built for nine queens, arranged so that none could tell which the Maharaja had chosen. Come at the end of the day, when the sandstone turns amber and the entire grid of the Pink City lays itself out below you.
